Land and water

Water

The Souss river has almost stopped flowing

Average flow at Aït Melloul fell from 6.6 m³/s in 1971–1999 to 1.4 m³/s since 2015, and was close to zero in 2022–2024. Rainfall at the upstream Youssef Ben Tachfine dam fell by about a fifth, while flow fell by four-fifths, so dams and irrigation pumping upstream likely explain much of the drop.

Oued Souss, mean annual flow at Aït Melloul (m³/s), by hydrological year
Agriculture

A drought year can cut the cereal harvest by two-thirds

Good years bring in around 10 million tonnes of cereals. In 2016, 2020 and 2022 the harvest fell to about 3.3–3.6 million. Five of the last twelve harvests came in under 6 million tonnes.

Work

Jobs

More than a third of young people looking for work can't find it

Unemployment among 15–24-year-olds rose from 27% in 2017 to 37% in 2025 (yearly averages). For young women it went from 35% to 53%. The national rate rose from 10.3% to 13.0%.

The survey method changed in 2017, which shows as a jump in the chart. Compare years on the same side of the dashed line.

Jobs

Graduates are six times more likely to be unemployed

At the end of 2025, one in four people with a higher-education degree was unemployed (25.7%), against 4.2% of people with no diploma. Most people without a diploma work informally, often in farming, so they rarely count as unemployed.

Regions

In the South, more than half of women in the labour force are unemployed

The Southern regions (22.8%) and the Oriental (22.1%) have nearly three times the unemployment of Marrakech-Safi (8.1%). The gap is widest for women.

Economy

Growth

The economy is growing 4–5% a year, after a 14–15% drop in spring 2020

Real GDP fell year on year by 14.9% in the second quarter of 2020, during the COVID-19 lockdown, according to HCP's first estimate; the revised series on the portal shows 14.1%. Since 2024 growth has held between 3.6% and 5.8%; it was 4.0% in the second quarter of 2026.

Real GDP, change on the same quarter a year earlier (seasonally adjusted, chained prices)
Credit

Bank lending is growing at its fastest pace since 2010

Bank credit to the economy grew 11.0% in the year to August 2026, to about 1.3 trillion dirhams. Between 2019 and 2025 it grew 4.8% a year on average.

Bank credit to the economy, change on a year earlier
Prices

Food inflation peaked at about 20%. Food prices are now falling

Year-on-year food inflation peaked in February 2023: HCP announced 20.1% at the time, and the revised index on the portal now gives 20.8%. By August 2026 food was 4.2% cheaper than a year earlier. Even so, meat costs 41% more than in 2017.

Rates are calculated year on year from HCP's published price index and may differ by a few tenths from HCP's announced figures.

Savings

Money held in investment funds has grown 77% since 2020

Net assets of Moroccan mutual funds (OPCVM) rose from 469 billion dirhams in January 2020 to 833 billion in November 2026.

Startups

More than two in three listed startups are in Casablanca or Rabat

The Digital Development Agency's 2024 database lists 802 different startups. 421 are in Casablanca and 127 in Rabat; no other city has more than 50.

Startups by city

Living

Health

Health insurance went from 1 in 5 Moroccans to 3 in 4

Mandatory health insurance (AMO) covered 19.7% of the population in 2012 and 77.1% in 2024. Most of the jump came in 2023–2024, after people on the RAMED medical-aid scheme were moved into AMO at the end of 2022.

Health insurance

Health insurance pays out five times more than in 2012, and still takes in more than it spends

The mandatory health insurance schemes (AMO) paid 27.2 billion dirhams in benefits in 2024, against 5.1 billion in 2012. Their total income was 46.1 billion and their total spending 40.9 billion. 2021 is the only year in which spending was higher than income.

IncomeSpending

Income includes contributions plus investment and other revenue; spending includes benefits, running costs and provisions. Billions of dirhams, current prices.

Public services

Casablanca-Settat has the fewest public health centres per resident

Morocco has 3,086 public health centres and rural dispensaries. Per 100,000 people, Guelmim-Oued Noun has 22; Casablanca-Settat, home to one Moroccan in five, has 4.9.

Public health centres and rural dispensaries per 100,000 people, 2025

These are counts of facilities, not of beds, staff or opening hours. City facilities tend to be larger, and private clinics, which are concentrated in big cities, are not included. Labs and specialist centres are left out. The ministry's hospital list does not include university hospitals, so hospitals are not compared here. Public schools show the same pattern. Population is from HCP's 2024 census.

Population: Population légale, RGPH 2024 (HCP)
Pensions

Fewer workers now pay in for each pensioner

In the basic pension schemes there were 6.6 contributors for each pensioner in 2012 and 4.8 in 2024. A falling ratio means each pension is backed by fewer contributions.

Contributors per pensioner, basic pension schemes
Pensions

Since 2017, the basic pension schemes have paid out more than they collect

In 2024 the basic schemes collected 73.0 billion dirhams in contributions and paid 78.7 billion in pensions. Their reserves peaked at 270.9 billion in 2021 and stood at 264.9 billion in 2024.

Connectivity

There are now more internet subscriptions than people

Internet subscriptions per 100 people went from 6 in 2010 to 113 in 2025, almost all on mobile. There are 161 mobile lines per 100 people, and 1.4 million fibre connections.

Road safety

Road deaths are climbing: 4,024 in 2024, about 11 a day

Deaths rose 15% between 2022 and 2024. Only one accident in five happens outside towns, yet those crashes cause more than half of all deaths.

People killed on Moroccan roads
Road safety

Vehicles are multiplying faster than road deaths

There were 4.5 million vehicles on Moroccan roads at the end of 2023, up from 3.1 million in 2012. Motorcycles grew almost ninefold, from 36,000 to 312,000. Deaths per 10,000 vehicles fell from 9.1 in 2021 to 8.5 in 2023.

State

Justice

Appeal courts receive 63% more cases than in 2021

Ordinary appeal courts registered 437,000 new cases in 2025, up from 269,000 in 2021. Across all courts, 4.1 million cases came in and 4.2 million were judged, so the system as a whole is keeping pace. Most of the appeal increase came in 2023 alone (+42%), which may reflect a change in how cases are counted rather than demand.

Integrity

Morocco has dropped 26 places in the corruption ranking since 2018

Transparency International's score peaked at 43/100 in 2018 (rank 73) and fell to 37 in 2024 (rank 99). A higher score means less perceived corruption.

Parliament

MPs asked 67,000 questions in seven years. Education, infrastructure and finance top the list

Between 2017 and early 2024, members of the House of Representatives sent 40,226 written and 26,888 oral questions to the government. Schools and universities drew the most, followed by roads, transport and water.

Questions by policy area, 2017–2024

Ministry names changed with the 2021 government, so questions are grouped into policy areas by the ministry they were addressed to. About 2% could not be grouped. Questions that appear in more than one session file are counted once.

About this page

Where the numbers come from

Every figure comes from data.gov.ma, Morocco's official open-data portal, last updated on 23 November 2026. The one exception is regional population, taken from HCP's published 2024 census results to compute per-resident rates. Each chart links to the dataset it uses. The scripts that turn the original spreadsheets into these charts are public in the GitHub repository, so every number can be checked and rebuilt.

Licence

The data is published under the Open Database License (ODbL). The processed figures behind this page are shared under the same licence in the repository's data folder. The page and scripts are under the MIT licence. Please credit data.gov.ma and the original publisher when you reuse the data.

Personal data

Some datasets on the portal list individuals by name, such as members of parliament or company founders. This page only shows counts and totals. It does not republish names, contact details or any other information about individuals.

How to read the findings

  • The figures describe what the data shows. They do not prove what caused it.
  • Where a series changes method, as HCP's employment survey did in 2017, the chart marks the break.
  • Some rates, such as inflation and growth, are calculated here from published indices and may differ by a few tenths from the official announcements.
  • This is an independent project. It is not produced or endorsed by the Moroccan government or by any publisher of the data.

Data checks

Every number in the text was recomputed from the original files, and headline figures were compared with official announcements. The audit of 24 November 2026 found and fixed these problems:

  • The startup database merges four overlapping lists: 1,028 rows describe 802 companies.
  • Parliamentary question files overlap: 2,939 questions appeared more than once.
  • The primary-care list includes labs and specialist centres, which are now excluded.
  • HCP's employment survey changed method in 2017, so comparisons start in 2017.
  • Food inflation and GDP for 2020 differ between first announcements and today's revised series; both are given.
