Prices: what is getting more expensive, and where

A monthly monitor of HCP's consumer price index (base 100 = 2017), nationally and in 18 cities. Latest month: August 2026. It updates when HCP publishes a new month.

Inflation

Prices are lower than a year ago

In August 2026, consumer prices were 0.3% lower than a year earlier, and food prices 4.2% lower. Over the last three months, with the usual seasonal pattern removed, prices moved at an annual rate of −2.8%. Inflation peaked at 10.1% in February 2023.

HCP revises its index. For February 2023 it announced food inflation of 20.1%; the index on the portal now gives 20.8%. All rates here are computed from today's index.

Drivers

What drove inflation in August 2026

Each bar is a group's share of the headline rate: its weight in the basket times its price change over the year. Transport added the most (+0.72 points). Food and soft drinks, 38% of the basket, contributed −1.74 points.

Contribution to year-on-year inflation, percentage points

HCP does not publish the basket weights on the portal, so they are estimated here from its own indices. They rebuild the headline index to within 0.07 index points, but fits on each half of the period differ by up to 3.8 points, so read individual weights as approximate.

Biggest movers

What moved most this month

Each product's monthly change is compared with its own history, using the median and median absolute deviation so that one past spike does not hide another. The biggest move in August 2026 was Bière: +3.6% in the month, 12 times its usual spread.

Forecast

The next three months

The ETS model beat the simple rule "same monthly changes as last year" in back-tests: its three-month-ahead error was 22% smaller (0.90 against 1.16 points). The test made a forecast from each of 36 months, June 2023 to May 2026, using only the data available at the time.

ModelAverage miss, points

A forecast is shown only if a model beats the benchmark of repeating last year's monthly changes. Forecasts are uncertain: the average miss is a guide to how far off they can be.

Method

  • Rates are computed from HCP's monthly index: year on year, month on month, and a three-month annualised rate on the seasonally adjusted index (robust STL decomposition).
  • Contributions are weight × price change ÷ last year's headline index, so they add up to the headline rate. The weights come from a non-negative least-squares fit of the headline index on its twelve groups.
  • Movers use a robust z-score of each product's monthly change against all its earlier months.
  • City groups come from hierarchical clustering on the correlation of seasonally adjusted monthly changes.
  • Forecasts: three models on the log index (repeat last year's monthly changes; exponential smoothing with a damped trend and seasonality; a seasonal ARIMA), tested from rolling origins three months ahead.

Limits

  • The index covers the cities HCP surveys; rural prices are not included.
  • HCP revises past months, so earlier figures here can differ from those announced at the time.
  • Weights are estimates, not HCP's official figures.
  • A model that beat the benchmark in the past can still miss, especially when prices turn quickly.
