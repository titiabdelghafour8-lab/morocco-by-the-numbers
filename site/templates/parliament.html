    Method

    • Questions. Every written and oral question file on the portal, with questions that appear in more than one file counted once (same number, kind and date). Topics use the subject line, which every question has.
    • Arabic preprocessing. Diacritics removed and letter variants normalised (as CAMeL Tools does), opening formulae removed, and common administrative words left out of topic keywords.
    • Topics. Each subject is turned into a vector by an Arabic language model (CAMeL-BERT, modern standard Arabic), then grouped with BERTopic (UMAP and HDBSCAN, fixed random seeds) and merged into {{topics_n}} topics. Questions that fit no group are given to the nearest topic.
    • Names. Each topic was named in Arabic, French and English after reading its keywords and sample questions. The names are kept in a reviewed file, and the site will not build if the model's topics change until the names are reviewed again.
    • Checks. Topic coherence (NPMI) {{coh_main}} and diversity {{div_main}}, against {{coh_base}} and {{div_base}} for a simple word-count baseline. As a check on meaning, a classifier that reads only the subject predicts which of {{clf_areas}} policy areas the question went to {{clf_acc}} of the time in {{clf_test}} (guessing the most common area: {{clf_base}}).

    Limits

    • Topics are statistical groups, named by a person. Another seed or model would draw some boundaries differently.
    • Subjects are short, and some mix several issues; each question gets exactly one topic.
    • A count of questions measures attention, not the importance of an issue or the quality of answers.
    • Years at the edges with few questions are left out of the shares.

    Personal data

    The question files name the member who asked each question. This page and its data keep no names, no parliamentary groups and no per-member counts. Sample subjects are screened against the portal's lists of members.
